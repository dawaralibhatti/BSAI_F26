Cathode Ray Oscilloscope

Cathode Ray Oscilloscope (CRO)

A CRO is an electronic device that draws a graph of a voltage signal against time. Think of it as a voltmeter that shows a picture.

Uses of a CRO (tap to open)

Circuit troubleshooting

See exactly where a signal is missing, weak or distorted in a circuit.

Component testing

Check parts like capacitors and transistors by watching how they affect a signal.

Measuring and manipulating AC signals

View the wave and stretch, shrink or move it on screen to read it properly.

Voltmeter / amplifier

Measures DC and AC voltage, and can magnify very small signals so they are visible.

Inside the tube

Electrons are shot at a screen in a vacuum tube and it glows where they hit. Tap any part to see its job.

Tap a part above. The beam starts at the cathode on the left and ends on the screen on the right.
Order the beam travels: cathode → grid → anodes → Y-plates → X-plates → screen

Functionality

Vertical axis (y) = potential difference (voltage)
Horizontal axis (x) = time

Why a wave appears

The input signal goes to the Y-plates, so the beam moves up and down with the voltage. The time base drags the beam across the screen left to right at constant speed, then snaps it back. Up/down plus left-to-right makes the voltage-time graph.

The two knobs that matter

KnobSetsFinds
VOLTS/DIVVolts per vertical squareVoltage, amplitude, Vpp
TIME/DIVTime per horizontal squarePeriod T, frequency f
Key idea: the screen shows divisions, not volts. Real value = divisions × knob setting.

Other controls (tap)

Brightness / Focus

Make the trace brighter or sharper.

Position knobs

Slide the trace up/down or left/right.

Trigger

Holds the wave still so it does not drift across the screen.

AC / DC / GND switch

DC shows the full signal. AC blocks the steady DC part. GND shows the zero line.

Measure it yourself

Steps
1. Count divisions tall, then × VOLTS/DIV = Vpp
2. Count divisions for one wave, then × TIME/DIV = T
3. f = 1 / T

Cheat sheet

QuantityFormula
Peak-to-peak voltageVpp = divy × (V/div)
AmplitudeV0 = Vpp / 2
Time periodT = divx × (time/div)
Frequencyf = 1 / T
f = 1 / T
Worked example
Wave 4 div tall at 0.5 V/div: Vpp = 2 V, amplitude = 1 V.
One wave is 5 div at 2 ms/div: T = 10 ms, f = 1 / 0.01 = 100 Hz.
Remember
• Y-axis = voltage, X-axis = time
• DC = flat line, AC = wave
• T in seconds gives f in Hz
